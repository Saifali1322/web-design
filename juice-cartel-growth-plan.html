The read

The product works. The machine around it doesn't exist.

Three things in the current numbers are genuinely strong, and they're worth naming because they're the things most food businesses never get:

  • 100% of customers are non-friends. Nobody is buying out of loyalty. That's real demand, and it means the thing scales past the personal network — which is where most hustles die.
  • 75% gross margin on both products. £1 → £4 on juice, £2 → £8 on cake. Those are excellent unit economics. Most food businesses fight for 60%.
  • Ramadan proved trend-jacking works. £1,000 in a month wasn't luck. It was the right product on the right occasion with the right content. That's repeatable.

What's missing is everything between the customer wanting it and the money landing: no website, no funnel, no card payments, no repeat mechanism. Every single month starts from zero and gets rebuilt by hand through DMs.

The core problem

The goal is MRR. There is currently no R.

"£1k profit MRR" is the right target, but nothing in the business today recurs. Every order is a fresh negotiation in a Snapchat DM, settled by bank transfer. That caps the business at however many conversations one person can have in a day — and it means the £600 has to be re-earned from scratch on the first of every month.

The fix isn't more orders. It's changing what's being sold: from a juice, to a weekly habit that's paid for in advance and delivered on a fixed day.

This does four things at once — and the ops win is as big as the revenue win:

  • Revenue becomes predictable. Month starts with money already committed.
  • COGS become predictable. Known quantities means near-zero waste, which is where a chunk of the missing margin is going.
  • One press, one route. Batch everything into a single delivery day instead of scattered drops all week.
  • Cash arrives before the cost. Prepaid subscriptions fund the ingredients.

Lever one

Cartel Club: the weekly drop

One delivery day a week. Pressed that morning, paid in advance, same customers every week. Three tiers, priced slightly below buying the same items à la carte so the subscription is obviously the better deal.

The shelf-life constraint shapes the product. Raw cold-pressed juice with no HPP treatment keeps roughly 3 days refrigerated — often quoted as a 72-hour maximum. That rules out a "week's worth in one drop." Build the tiers around 3-day packs and be straight about it on the label: keep refrigerated, drink within 3 days. Honesty here is also brand — it's the proof it's genuinely raw.

Lever two

Find the missing 25 points of margin

Unit economics say 75% gross margin. Reality is delivering 50%. That 25-point gap is roughly £150 a month disappearing into packaging, fuel, waste and sub-scale ingredient buying — and closing even half of it is the cheapest money available, because it needs no new customers at all.

Lever three

Plug the holes between wanting it and paying

Bank transfer and cash only, with no website, is the single biggest conversion leak in the business. Every stranger who sees a TikTok has to DM, wait for a reply, agree a price, get sort code and account number, and manually push money to a person they've never met. A meaningful share of interested people fall out at each of those steps — and unlike content, none of this needs an audience to fix.

  • Card payments, this week. Stripe, SumUp or Square gets card and Apple Pay live in a day. Card fees of ~1.5% are trivial next to the orders currently lost to transfer friction.
  • A one-page ordering site. Menu, prices, postcodes served, delivery day, checkout. One link in every bio, every Snap, every video. It replaces the entire DM negotiation.
  • Subscriptions on Direct Debit. Start on Stripe for speed. At scale, GoCardless charges roughly 1% and fails far less often than cards, which matters when the same charge repeats weekly.
  • A link in every single post. Content currently sends people to a dead end. Every view should have somewhere to go.
Demand

Two posts a week is the biggest under-investment in the business

Local food content on TikTok needs 5–7 posts a week to hold algorithmic momentum — two is roughly a quarter of the required volume, which is why growth feels flat. The good news is that filming is nearly free and he's already the actor, which is the hard part most people can't solve.

  • Batch-film on press day. The weekly press run is the content. Twenty minutes with a phone on a tripod during a session he's doing anyway yields a week of posts.
  • Stop posting the same video to both platforms. Both platforms suppress content that's obviously recycled. Re-record the hook and change the cover — same shoot, two edits.
  • Write hooks, not scripts. The first 1.5 seconds decides everything. ChatGPT is fine for this, but it should be generating twenty competing hooks to test, not one full script to read aloud.
  • Lean into Snapchat. It's already the top channel and it's the least contested. Public Stories, Spotlight and Snap Map put content in front of the local area specifically.
  • Build a trend calendar. Ramadan wasn't a fluke, it was an occasion with a matching product. January detox, exam season, heatwaves, Eid, Valentine's, Mother's Day — plan the SKU before the date, not during it.

Two channels that outperform content per hour spent

Referral, printed on card. Every delivery goes out with "£3 off your next order, and £3 for whoever you send." With a 75% margin, £6 to acquire a customer who might subscribe for months is cheap. Unique codes make it measurable.

Local partnerships. One gym with 200 members allowing a Saturday sampling table is worth more than a hundred videos. The same applies to PT studios, barbers, salons, offices wanting a Friday drop, and community centres during Ramadan. These convert into subscribers far faster than cold social traffic because there's trust and physical proof of the product.

Before scaling

Four things that can end this business

Growing a food business without these handled doesn't just risk a fine — it risks the whole thing being shut down at exactly the moment it starts working.

Not registered with the council

Registration is free, cannot be refused, and is legally required at least 28 days before trading. Trading without it is an offence. Home kitchens carry the same obligations as commercial premises. This is a one-evening fix and it's the highest-priority item on the entire plan.

Allergen labelling on the desserts

A mini egg crunch cake almost certainly contains milk, wheat, soya and possibly nuts. For food sold at distance, allergen information must be available both at the point of order and again at delivery. This is the one where getting it wrong can seriously hurt somebody.

Overstating shelf life

Raw juice is 3 days refrigerated, not a week. Label it honestly, deliver chilled in cool bags with ice packs, and design the tiers around 72 hours. One person getting ill from a four-day-old bottle is worse than any growth this plan produces.

One person is every role

He presses, films, acts, sells and drives. At 23 subscribers that's sustainable. Somewhere around 50–60 it breaks, and it breaks first as missed deliveries — the fastest way to lose a subscription base. Decide the handoff point in advance: delivery is the first thing to give away, content is the last.

Where the leverage is

If only three things get done

  • Take card payments and put up an ordering page. Costs almost nothing, converts traffic that's already arriving, and unblocks everything else in this plan.
  • Launch the weekly subscription and sell it to existing customers. They've already bought once. Eight of them signing up is a third of the entire goal.
  • Go from 2 posts a week to 5, batch-filmed in one session. The content already works — there just isn't enough of it to compound.

The business doesn't need reinventing. The product sells to strangers at a 75% margin already, which is the part most people never reach. What it needs is a way to take money, a reason for customers to come back on a schedule, and enough content volume for the algorithm to notice.
